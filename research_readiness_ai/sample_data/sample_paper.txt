Abstract
We propose a novel framework for predicting customer churn using a hybrid
gradient boosting and deep learning architecture. Unlike prior work that
relies solely on tabular features, our approach fuses tabular and behavioral
sequence data. We evaluate on three public benchmark datasets and show that
our method outperforms the state-of-the-art baseline by 4.2% F1 score. Our
contribution includes a new feature fusion layer and a reproducible training
pipeline with released code.

Introduction
Customer churn prediction is a critical business problem [1][2]. Prior
approaches such as logistic regression and random forests [3] often ignore
temporal behavior. In contrast to previous work, we introduce a fusion
architecture that combines XGBoost-derived tabular embeddings with an LSTM
over user event sequences. For the first time, we show this joint
representation improves recall on minority (churned) classes without
degrading precision.

Related Work
Several methods have been proposed for churn prediction (Zhang et al., 2022;
Lee et al., 2023). Traditional machine learning approaches [4][5] use manual
feature engineering. Deep learning approaches (Chen, 2021) use raw sequences
but suffer from limited interpretability. Our work builds on both lines by
using SHAP explainability on top of a hybrid model.

Methodology
Our proposed architecture consists of two branches. Branch A is an XGBoost
model trained on tabular features (demographics, account tenure, spend).
Branch B is a bidirectional LSTM over the last 90 days of user events. We
concatenate the outputs and feed them to a small feedforward classifier.
Hyperparameters: learning rate 1e-3, batch size 64, 30 epochs, Adam
optimizer. Code and trained weights will be released on GitHub upon
acceptance to support reproducibility.

Experiments
We evaluate on three datasets: Telco Churn, Bank Churn, and a proprietary
retail dataset. Table 1 reports Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1 against
three baselines: Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and a standalone
XGBoost model. Figure 1 shows the training curves. Our method achieves an
F1 of 0.81 versus 0.777 for the strongest baseline (XGBoost only), a

removing the LSTM branch, confirming its contribution to recall.

Discussion
The results confirm our hypothesis that temporal behavioral signals
meaningfully improve churn prediction beyond static tabular features.
Limitations include the moderate size of the proprietary dataset and
possible domain shift when applied to industries outside retail and telecom.

Conclusion
We presented a hybrid fusion architecture for churn prediction that
outperforms strong baselines while remaining interpretable via SHAP. Future
work includes extending the framework to multi-task settings and testing on
additional industry verticals.
